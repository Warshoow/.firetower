# False positives and what to preserve

Read this before editing anyone else's writing. A clean human writer hits several catalog patterns without any AI involvement, and over-editing destroys exactly what made the text sound like a person.

The rule underneath both lists: look for **clusters**, not isolated hits. One em dash proves nothing. Em dashes plus rule of three plus "vibrant tapestry" plus a "Conclusion" section is a confession.

## What not to flag

None of these is evidence on its own.

**Perfect grammar and consistent style.** Many writers are professionals or have been edited. Polish is not AI.

**Mixed casual and formal registers.** This usually signals a person in a technical field, a young writer, a non-native speaker, or someone with distinctive prose habits. Not a chatbot.

**Bland or robotic prose.** AI prose has *specific* tells. Generic dryness without those tells is just dry writing, and flattening it further makes it worse.

**Formal or academic vocabulary.** Pattern 7 lists specific words that spike in AI text. It is not a licence to simplify every long word. Leave "ostensibly" and "constituent" alone.

**Letter-style openings and sign-offs.** Salutations predate ChatGPT by centuries.

**A single transition word.** Additionally, moreover, and consequently are AI-coded only when piled up. One "however" is not a tell.

**Curly quotes alone.** macOS, Word, Google Docs, and most CMSes auto-curl by default.

**Em dashes alone.** Many editors and journalists use them constantly. Dashes count only when paired with the sales-y rhythm.

**One short sentence for emphasis.** Flag dramatic fragments only when several appear in a row.

**Deliberate repeated openings.** "She came. She saw. She conquered." Repetition used for rhythm or pressure stays.

**"Honestly" or "look" mid-sentence.** Ordinary in casual writing. The tell is the standalone theatrical opener.

**Real limits, disclaimers, and objections.** Keep scope statements, legal and safety notices, genuine corrections, named objections and their replies, and FAQ answers. Patterns 34 and 35 target *unsupported* defenses and *fake* alternatives, not real ones.

**Real alternatives.** In a design document, a tutorial, or an argument, options the reader would actually consider belong in the text.

**Unsourced claims.** Most of the web is unsourced. Lack of citations proves nothing about authorship.

**Correct, complex formatting.** Visual editors and templates produce clean output with no AI involved.

**Secondhand text.** Never rewrite a watched phrase inside a quotation, a title, a proper name, or an example where the phrase is being discussed rather than used.

**Anything written before 30 November 2022.** ChatGPT's public launch. With rare exceptions, older text is not AI-written.

## Human details to keep

When these appear, lean toward leaving the prose alone.

**Specific, unusual, hard-to-fabricate detail.** A real address. An odd quote. "The lawyer who used to work upstairs from my dentist." Models round off specifics; people hoard them.

**Mixed feelings and unresolved tension.** "I think this is mostly good, but it bothers me, and I can't fully explain why." Models default to clean takes.

**Dated, era-bound references.** Slang, memes, and in-jokes that map to a specific year and subculture. Models lag by a year or more.

**First-person choices the writer can defend.** If they can explain why they made that cut or used that word, that is a strong human signal.

**Variety in sentence length.** Real writing alternates short and long. AI writing tends toward an even mid-length cadence.

**Genuine asides, parentheticals, and self-corrections.** "(I keep wanting to say 'almost' here, but it really was certain.)" Models rarely interrupt themselves.

## How to report

Diagnose, then fix, then explain. List which patterns appear, by number. Rewrite with them removed. Note briefly what changed and why.

Describe the pattern, not the author. "This paragraph has a significance tail and a false range" is useful. "This was written by AI" is a claim you cannot support and do not need to make.
